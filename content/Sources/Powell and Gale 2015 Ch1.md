# Powell and Gale 2015 Ch1

> [!summary] In plain words
> A friendly opening chapter on what a model is, the kinds of models ecologists build, why we model at all, and Starfield's step-by-step modeling process.

**Citation:** Powell, L. A., and G. A. Gale. 2015. *Estimation of Parameters for Animal Populations: a primer for the rest of us.* Ch. 1, "Models of populations", pp. 1 to 10. Caught Napping Publications, Lincoln, NE.
**Syllabus week:** Tue Aug 18, "What is 'modeling?' and WHY model?"

## What it's about
It starts with the balls-in-a-room exercise to show that you already build models, then sets out the Real World / Model World framework, model types, reasons to model, the seven-step process, and when you should and shouldn't model.

## Key ideas I use
- A model is "a simplification of the real world that allows study of the real world", and all models are wrong, some useful (p. 1) → [[Model]]
- Balls in the room: the model's structure depends on its purpose (pp. 2 to 4) → [[Model]]
- Real World → Model World → Model, with Data from the Real World (Fig. 1.4, pp. 4 to 5) → [[Real World and Model World]]
- $N_{t+1} = N_t r$, with r = b − d (p. 5) → [[Analytical Model]]
- Conceptual, physical, graphical, analytical and statistical models (pp. 5 to 6) → [[Conceptual Model]], [[Analytical Model]], [[Statistical Model]]
- Survival estimator S = n_alive / n_tagged, and the sample mean as statistical models (p. 6) → [[Statistical Model]], [[Parameter vs Estimate]]
- Four reasons to model (pp. 6 to 7) → [[Why model at all]]
- Seven modeling steps, including sensitivity analysis and an assumptions check (p. 7) → [[Modeling Process]], [[Sensitivity Analysis]]
- Should you model? It depends on data and understanding (Fig. 1.6, p. 8) → [[Why model at all]]
- BIDE: ΔN = B − D + I − E (p. 9) → [[Analytical Model]]

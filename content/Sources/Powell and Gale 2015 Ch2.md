# Powell and Gale 2015 Ch2

> [!summary] In plain words
> Why estimates exist (to inform management and science), what population our conclusions really cover, the two kinds of reasoning, and how to describe an estimate's quality with bias and precision.

**Citation:** Powell, L. A., and G. A. Gale. 2015. *Estimation of Parameters for Animal Populations: a primer for the rest of us.* Ch. 2, "Context and inference", pp. 11 to 20. Caught Napping Publications, Lincoln, NE.
**Syllabus week:** Tue Aug 18, "What is 'modeling?' and WHY model?"

> [!warning] Verify
> The chapter lists "binomial coefficients" among its topics, but in my copy that section appears in Ch3 (pp. 22 to 24). The binomial notes cite Ch3.

## What it's about
The BIDE model of population change, the management cycle and adaptive management, biological vs statistical populations, representative samples, deduction vs induction, and bias vs precision.

## Key ideas I use
- BIDE population model (Fig. 2.1, p. 11) → [[Analytical Model]]
- The management cycle mirrors the scientific method: characterizations, hypotheses, predictions, experiments (pp. 12 to 13) → [[Hypothesis]]
- Biological vs statistical population; sampling units; the turtle trap example (pp. 13 to 14) → [[Biological vs Statistical Population]], [[Sampling Unit]]
- The sample must be representative; the blind men and the elephant (pp. 14 to 15) → [[Representative Sample]], [[Population and Sample]]
- Deduction vs induction; "induction is central to the study of statistical inference" (pp. 15 to 16) → [[Induction vs Deduction]]
- Parameter (θ), estimate (θ̂), estimator (definitions box, p. 16) → [[Parameter vs Estimate]]
- Bias and precision; small-sample vs model bias; avoid "accuracy"; value lack of bias over precision (pp. 16 to 18, Fig. 2.5) → [[Bias and Precision]]

# Powell and Gale 2015 Ch3

> [!summary] In plain words
> Maximum likelihood explained slowly: write the probability of the data as a function of the unknown parameter, then find the value that makes the observed data most probable.

**Citation:** Powell, L. A., and G. A. Gale. 2015. *Estimation of Parameters for Animal Populations: A Primer for the Rest of Us.* Caught Napping Publications, Lincoln, NE. Ch. 3, "Maximum likelihood estimation".
**Syllabus week:** Tue Sep 15, "Model selection".

## What it's about
Starting from coin tosses and a fish-tagging example, the chapter builds a likelihood from the binomial distribution (with binomial coefficients and products), shows the maximum by plotting and by calculus, gives a "maximum likelihood cookbook", and estimates variance from the curvature of the likelihood.

## Key ideas I use
- Likelihood as the probability of the data given a parameter → [[Likelihood Function]]
- Binomial coefficient and product notation in likelihoods → [[Binomial Coefficient]], [[Product Notation]]
- Maximum likelihood estimate by search or calculus → [[Maximum Likelihood Estimation]]
- Log-likelihood makes products into sums → [[Log-likelihood]]
- Variance from the likelihood's curvature → [[Variance of an Estimate]]

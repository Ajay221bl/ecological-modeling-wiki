## What it's about
Two Santa Fe Institute scientists pass on the novelist Cormac McCarthy's editing advice for science writing: keep it simple, tell one clear story, and make life easy for the reader.

## Key ideas I use
- Minimalism: cut any word, sentence or section the message doesn't need; pick one theme and two or three points every reader should remember (p. 441) -> [[Project Overview]], [[Hypotheses]]
- One message per paragraph; short, direct sentences (p. 441)
- Avoid jargon and don't over-elaborate (p. 442). When jargon is needed, define it, as this vault does for terms like [[Overdispersion]]
- Choose concrete language and examples, and keep equations out of the middle of sentences (p. 442) -> [[Interpreting Poisson Coefficients]] (write "about 1.4 times as many species per visit as BK" rather than only quoting a log-scale coefficient; 1.40 is CR's rate ratio in the Oct 13 top model, [[Preliminary Results]])
- Read the draft aloud and find an editor you trust (p. 442) -> [[Milestones and Deadlines]]

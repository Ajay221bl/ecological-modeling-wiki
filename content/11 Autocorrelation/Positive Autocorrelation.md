# Positive Autocorrelation

**Positive autocorrelation** means nearby observations are more similar than random pairs, producing patches in space or smooth runs in time, as in the left panel of the figure ([[Autocorrelation]]). Moran's I is above its [[Expected Value|expected value]] of −1/(n − 1), [[Geary's c]] is below 1, and ACF bars are above zero ([[Moran's I]]). It is by far the most common kind in ecology, caused by a patchy environment, local dispersal, clumped social behaviour or slow population change; positive autocorrelation of abundance indicates clumping.

For ordinary models it makes standard errors too small and so p-values too small ([[Inflated Type I Error]]). The distance up to which it persists estimates [[Landscape Metrics|patch size]], as Diniz-Filho et al. use it ([[Diniz-Filho et al. 2003]], [[Dynamic Ecology Autocorrelation Post]]).

Positive autocorrelation is not a positive correlation between x and y; it is a variable correlated with itself. Its opposite is [[Negative Autocorrelation]].

# Phylogenetic Autocorrelation

**Phylogenetic autocorrelation** means closely related species have similar traits because they share recent ancestors, so species are not independent data points ([[Autocorrelation]], [[Independence of Observations|Independence]]). Here "distance" is the time since two species shared an ancestor. Body size, life history and many other traits show it.

Comparative analyses across species must account for it, or sister species count as independent evidence when they are partly copies. The tools are phylogenetic generalised least squares (PGLS), which is [[Generalized Least Squares]] with a correlation matrix built from the tree ([[Correlation Structure]]), and phylogenetic random effects in mixed models. Weak phylogenetic signal suggests a trait is evolutionarily labile.

The [[Dynamic Ecology Autocorrelation Post]] lists phylogeny alongside space and time as sources of autocorrelation. Species are not independent replicates: related species share history.

# Tobler's First Law

**Tobler's first law of geography**, from the geographer Waldo Tobler (1970), says "everything is related to everything else, but near things are more related than distant things". It is the plain-language statement of positive [[Spatial Autocorrelation]], and McGill notes the same holds in ecology across space, time and phylogeny ([[Autocorrelation]]).

It is a tendency, not a law of nature: [[Negative Autocorrelation|negative autocorrelation]] and barriers such as rivers and mountains break it. A [[Correlogram]] shows how far "near" extends in a given dataset.

It is the opening line of the [[Dynamic Ecology Autocorrelation Post]]. It does not say distant things are unrelated, only that they are less related.

# One Idea per Paragraph

A kitchen has one purpose; if you also put a bed in it, visitors are confused about where they are. Each **paragraph** should likewise carry one message, usually introduced by its first sentence, so readers can follow the argument by reading first sentences alone ([[Theme and Key Points]]). McCarthy (Savage and Yeh 2019) says to limit each paragraph to a single message, and a single sentence can be a paragraph. A paragraph can move from a question to an idea and sometimes to an answer, and it is fine to leave a question open.

The first sentence states the point and the rest supports it. To check, read only the first sentence of every paragraph: together they should read as a summary of the paper. In results, use one paragraph per question or model, in the order of the methods ([[Writing Results Sections]], [[Writing Methods Sections]]).

It comes from [[McCarthy Writing Advice]], alongside [[Plain Clear Sentences]]. Long paragraphs do not look more scholarly; they hide the point.

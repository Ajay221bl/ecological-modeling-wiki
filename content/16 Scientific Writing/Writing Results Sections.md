# Writing Results Sections

A **results section** reports what the analysis found, effect sizes with uncertainty first and [[Test Statistic|test statistics]] second, in the order of the questions, pointing to figures and tables without interpreting beyond the data ([[Effect Size]], [[Confidence Interval]], [[Writing Methods Sections]]). Compare "Forest cover was significant (p < 0.05)" with "Occupancy increased with forest cover, from 0.21 (95% CI 0.12 to 0.33) at 10% cover to 0.78 (0.64 to 0.88) at 90% (Fig. 2)". Lead with the ecological answer in units readers understand, and give the estimate and interval before any p-value ([[P-value]]).

[[Back-transformation|Back-transform]] from log or [[Logit Link|logit]] scales, or show predictions on the [[Response Scale|response scale]] ([[Predictions on Link vs Response Scale]]). Follow the order of the methods, one paragraph per question ([[One Idea per Paragraph]]). Figures carry the main result, and the text states what the reader should see in them ([[Figures for Model Results]]). Report briefly what didn't work or was not supported; [[Harrison et al. 2018]] recommend reporting full (global) model estimates, which helps future meta-analyses. Save interpretation and comparison with other studies for the discussion.

This is from the November 24 writing-results session ([[Course Website]], [[McCarthy Writing Advice]], [[Reporting Statistics in Text]]). Results are not a list of p-values; they describe the size and direction of effects with uncertainty.

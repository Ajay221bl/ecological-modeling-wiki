# Writing Results Sections

**In one line:** a results section reports what the analysis found, effect sizes with uncertainty first and test statistics second, in the order of the questions, pointing to figures and tables without interpreting beyond the data.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Effect Size]] · [[Confidence Interval]] · [[Writing Methods Sections]]

## A tiny example
Weak: "Forest cover was significant (p < 0.05)."
Better: "Occupancy increased with forest cover, from 0.21 (95% CI 0.12 to 0.33) at 10% cover to 0.78 (0.64 to 0.88) at 90% (Fig. 2)."

## Start from zero
1. **Lead with the ecological answer,** in units readers understand.
2. **Effect size + uncertainty** (estimate and interval) before any p-value.
3. **Back-transform** from log or logit scales, or show predictions on the response scale.
4. **Follow the methods' order;** one paragraph per question.
5. **Figures carry the main result;** text states what the reader should see in them.
6. **Report what didn't work** or was not supported, briefly; Harrison et al. (2018) recommend reporting full (global) model estimates, which helps future meta-analyses.
7. **Save interpretation and comparison with other studies for the discussion.**

# Population and Sample

> [!summary] In plain words
> The **population** is everything you want to know about; the **sample** is the part you actually measured. Statistics is the craft of saying something honest about the first using only the second.

## The everyday picture
Tasting soup. The pot is the population; the spoonful is the sample. You never drink the whole pot, but one well-stirred spoonful tells you if it needs salt. An unstirred spoonful from the top might only taste the oil.

*Where the picture stops working:* soup can be stirred. Ecologists can't stir a landscape, so they randomise where they sample instead.

## The question
How does a sample relate to its population, and why does that link need care?

## Built from
- [[Population]]: the whole set of units you want to describe
- [[Sample]]: the units you actually measured
- [[Representative Sample]]: a sample that resembles its population

## See it
![[Population and sample.png|700]]
*What to look at:* the sample is a small subset; the question is whether it looks like the whole.

## The reasoning, step by step
1. **You care about the population** but can rarely measure all of it ([[Population]]).
2. **So you measure a sample** ([[Sample]]).
3. **Sample numbers estimate population numbers.** *Why only estimate:* a different sample would give a slightly different answer ([[Sampling Variation]]).
4. **The sample only speaks for the population it was drawn from.** *Why:* units that could never be picked contribute nothing ([[Statistical Population]]).

**A worked example in numbers.** A lake holds 10,000 fish (population). You net 50 (sample) and find mean length 21 cm. That 21 cm is your best guess for the lake's mean. Net another 50 and you might get 22 cm. If your net misses fish under 10 cm, both guesses are too high for the whole lake.

## So in practice
Say exactly which population you mean, then check that every member had a chance of being sampled.

## What would go wrong otherwise
- You'd report a sample number as if it were the population's true value, with no uncertainty.
- You'd generalise to units your method could never reach.

## Explain it back
- Use the soup to explain population and sample.
- Why can't a sample answer a question about units it could never include?

*Pass test:* for any study, you can name the population and the sample separately.

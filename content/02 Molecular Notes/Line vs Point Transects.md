# Line vs Point Transects

> [!summary] In plain words
> line transects record perpendicular distances from a travelled line and suit open terrain and conspicuous animals, while point transects record radial distances from a fixed point and suit dense habitat and animals detected by sound.

## The question
Should I use line transects or point counts with distances?

## Built from
- [[Line Transects]] and [[Point Transects]].
- [[Effective Strip Width]] and [[Detection Function]].

## The reasoning, step by step
1. **Geometry:** a line surveys a strip (area grows linearly with distance); a point surveys circles (area grows with distance squared).
2. **Precision:** lines gather more detections per hour in open habitat.
3. **Practicality:** points are easier in rough or dense terrain and for multi-species bird counts.
4. **Distance accuracy matters more for points,** because errors are squared in the area.
5. **Movement during the count** is worse for points (longer time at one place).

| | Line | Point |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | Perpendicular | Radial |
| Effective area | 2 × ESW × L | π × EDR² × points |
| Suits | Open terrain, aerial, marine | Forest, songbirds |
| Main risk | Animals flee ahead | Movement, distance error |

## So in practice
Songbirds in forest: points. Ungulates on savanna or whales at sea: lines.

## What would go wrong otherwise
Using point counts for large animals that flush as the observer arrives.

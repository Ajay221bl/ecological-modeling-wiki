# Breeding Season Timing

Open a window at dawn in May and the air is full of song; open it in late August and it is much quieter, though many of the same birds are still around. What changed is the **breeding season**, the window each year when birds pair up and raise young. In spring a male claims a *territory*, a patch he defends against other males of his species, and singing is how he claims it and attracts a mate. Then come nest building, eggs, chicks and *fledging* (young birds leaving the nest), and once nesting is done singing drops off sharply. *Residents* such as the [[Northern Bobwhite]] live in one area all year and start calling as the season warms; *migrants* such as the [[Dickcissel]], [[Eastern Kingbird]] and [[Scissor-tailed Flycatcher]] arrive from the south in spring and leave in late summer. The calendar of such seasonal events in a region is its [[Phenology]].

Timing matters because a [[Point Count Survey]], counting birds by sight and sound from a fixed spot, works only while birds are on territory, singing and staying put. Work through the numbers. Say a [[Eastern Meadowlark|meadowlark]] at a point sings during a count 60% of the time in early June but only 30% in mid-July. With three visits, the chance of detecting it at least once is one minus the chance of missing it all three times. In June that is 1 − 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.4 = 1 − 0.064 = 94%; in July it is 1 − 0.7³ = 1 − 0.343 = 66%. Same bird, same place, and counting six weeks later loses about a third of the detections. That is a change in [[Detection Probability]], the chance of noticing a bird that is there, and a bird missed this way is a case of [[Imperfect Detection]]. Counting too early has the opposite problem: some birds are still migrating through, so a bird heard on May 10 may be gone by May 20, and that breaks the rule of an [[Occupancy Model]] that a point's occupied or empty status does not change between the visits of a [[Repeat-Visit Design]]. So surveys such as the [[Breeding Bird Survey]] run mostly in June, early in the morning when singing peaks. Management has to fit the calendar too: [[Mowing]] or [[Prescribed Fire]] in the middle of nesting can destroy ground nests.

The common confusion is "a bird not heard in July was not there"; it may have finished nesting and gone quiet. Residents need timing too, since bobwhite calling also rises and falls, and warmer springs do more than shift dates: they can put birds out of step with insect peaks ([[Climate Change Effects on Wildlife]]). In the grassland bird project the counts at BK, DC and HO ran June 6 to July 12, 2022 (BK June 24 to July 12), CR was counted May 16 to 22 and HB June 1 to 9, 2023, from 30 minutes before sunrise to 5 hours after, with wind under 19.3 km/h and no rain. Date is therefore tangled with site, just as year is ([[Why site and year cannot be separated]]): a difference between CR and BK could partly be a seasonal difference in singing or in which migrants had arrived, which is [[Confounding]]. For example, 6 of the 9 Eastern Kingbird visits came from CR, and all 8 [[Loggerhead Shrike]] visits came from 2022 sites. Date is a natural [[Detection Covariates|detection covariate]], something that changes the chance of detection rather than whether the bird is there. The sites were also often partly flooded into early summer, one reason the study built a [[Flood Masking|flood mask]].

## Flashcards

Why are breeding-bird point counts done in early summer mornings?
??
Birds are on territory and singing most then, so a bird that is present is most likely to be detected, and migrants passing through have mostly moved on.
++++

A bird is detected on 60% of visits in June but 30% in July. What is the chance of detecting it at least once in 3 visits in each month?
??
June: 1 − 0.4³ = 94%. July: 1 − 0.7³ = 66%.
++++

CR was counted in mid-May and BK in late June to July. Why does that make site comparisons harder?
??
Singing and which migrants are present change through the season, so a difference between CR and BK could come from the date of counting rather than the sites, and with one window per site the two cannot be separated.

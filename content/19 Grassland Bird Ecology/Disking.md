# Disking

Picture a row of dinner plates set on edge and pulled through the ground: each plate cuts in and flips a ribbon of soil. That is a *disk harrow*, a farm tool for breaking up ground before planting, and **disking** is pulling one behind a tractor. Wildlife managers borrow it as *light disking*, a shallow pass in strips rather than ploughing a whole field. The dead mat of grass is chopped in, bare soil appears between plants, and seeds that were lying dormant in the soil (the *seed bank*) get light and sprout, mostly fast-growing annual weeds and wildflowers such as ragweed and partridge pea. Within weeks the strip looks like a young, weedy old field. Disking is a [[Disturbance]], an event that removes or damages living plants, and a strong one, because it reaches the soil.

Why use it? Every disturbance resets [[Ecological Succession]], the natural sequence from bare soil through annual weeds, perennial grasses and shrubs to forest, but by different amounts. On a field already growing shrubs and saplings, [[Prescribed Fire]] sets it back to about the grass stage (grass survives, small woody stems are killed), [[Mowing]] to grass-or-shrub (woody plants resprout from roots), and disking right back to bare soil and annual weeds, because it tears up roots. That makes it good for three jobs. It creates brood habitat for quail: [[Northern Bobwhite]] chicks eat insects and need to walk between plants, and a disked strip has bugs, seeds and bare ground at chick height. It adds variety: say a 4 ha field is split into four 1 ha strips and one strip is disked each year in rotation; after four years the field always holds strips of four different ages, a [[Multi-panel Figures|patchwork]] that suits more species ([[Habitat Selection]]). And a disked line has no fuel, so it works as a firebreak for a prescribed burn. The downsides: it can favour invasive weeds, cause erosion on slopes and damage native perennial grasses if overdone.

Three confusions. Light disking is not ploughing, which turns the whole field deeply. Disking does not remove trees: a disk will not cut through a 20 cm trunk, only seedlings and small stems. And more is not always better, since too much turns grassland into weedy bare ground that [[Habitat Specialist|grassland specialists]] avoid ([[Grassland Specialists vs Generalists]]). In the grassland bird project, the metadata list disking among the restoration treatments at all five sites ([[Roberts et al. 2025]], [[Grassland Restoration and Management]]), and the habitat file's `OF50` (percent old field) and `IA50` (percent inactive [[Land-Use Change and Agriculture|agriculture]]) describe land at roughly the stage disking creates. HO had the most Northern Bobwhite point-visits of any site (91, against 28 to 40 at BK, CR and DC and 7 at HB), and Berry's thesis summary says the Cache River tracts (DC and HO) are often disked and mowed (to confirm with Dr. Roberts). That is a story to test, not a finding: one site, tied to one year, with many other differences ([[Correlation vs Causation]], [[Confounding]]).

## Flashcards

Why does disking reset succession further back than mowing does?
??
Mowing only cuts the tops, so roots, including woody roots, survive and regrow. Disking tears into the soil and roots, leaving bare ground where annual weeds sprout from the seed bank, so the field restarts near the first stage.
++++

Why is disking a classic bobwhite management practice?
??
Disked strips grow annual weeds full of insects and seeds, with bare ground between plants, which is what bobwhite chicks need to feed and move.
++++

# Post hoc Analysis

**In one line:** a post hoc analysis is one devised after seeing the data or results, which can generate new hypotheses but can't test them with the same data.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Candidate Model Set]] (the a priori alternative) · [[Data Dredging]] (what it becomes if disguised)

## Start from zero
1. **A priori:** planned before the data. Its results are confirmatory.
2. **Post hoc:** suggested by the data. Its results are exploratory.
3. **Post hoc results look stronger than they are,** because the data that suggested them also "confirm" them.
4. **Fine and valuable if labelled honestly:** "an exploratory analysis suggested...".
5. **Test post hoc ideas on new data,** or a held-out portion.
6. **"Post hoc tests" after ANOVA** (Tukey, for example) are a related but narrower usage: pairwise comparisons after an overall test, with corrections for multiple comparisons.

## In the course
[[Anderson and Burnham 2002]] separate a priori and post hoc results.

## Common confusions
- **"Post hoc analysis is forbidden."** It is how new ideas arise. Presenting it as confirmatory is the problem.

## Explain it back
- How should post hoc results be presented?

*Pass test:* you can label analyses as a priori or post hoc.

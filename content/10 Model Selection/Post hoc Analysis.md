# Post hoc Analysis

A **post hoc analysis** is one devised after seeing the data or results. An a priori analysis is planned before the data and its results are confirmatory; a post hoc one is suggested by the data and its results are exploratory ([[Candidate Model Set]], [[Exploratory Modeling]]). Post hoc results look stronger than they are, because the data that suggested them also "confirm" them.

They are fine and valuable if labelled honestly, as in "an exploratory analysis suggested...", and should be tested on new data or a held-out portion ([[Cross-validation]]). Disguised as confirmatory, they become [[Data Dredging]]. "Post hoc tests" after ANOVA, such as Tukey's, are a related but narrower usage: pairwise comparisons after an overall test, with corrections for [[Pairwise Comparisons|Multiple Comparisons]].

[[Anderson and Burnham 2002]] keep a priori and post hoc results separate. Post hoc analysis is not forbidden: it is how new ideas arise, and only presenting it as confirmatory is the problem.

# Strong Inference

In a tournament bracket each round is designed so that some contenders must lose, and progress is fast because every match eliminates someone. **Strong inference** is Platt's (1964) method of making science progress that way. Devise alternative hypotheses ([[Multiple Working Hypotheses]]); devise a crucial experiment whose outcomes would exclude one or more of them; carry it out cleanly; then recycle, refining the surviving hypotheses and repeating ([[Hypothesis]], [[Hypothetico-deductive Method]]). Platt argued that fields working this way, molecular biology at the time, advance much faster than fields that test one favoured idea at a time.

In ecology crucial experiments are often impossible. [[Model Selection]] is a substitute: each hypothesis becomes a model in the [[Candidate Model Set]], and the data weigh them all at once. The spirit carries over, comparing explicit alternatives rather than testing one idea against "nothing happens".

It is discussed in the model selection lecture and in [[Johnson and Omland 2004]] ([[Course Website]]). Strong inference is not "strong statistical evidence": it is a strategy for designing research, not a measure of evidence.

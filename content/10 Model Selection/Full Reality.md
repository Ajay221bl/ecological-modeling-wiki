# Full Reality

A coastline's true length depends on the size of your ruler and keeps growing as the ruler shrinks; every map is a simplification, and the question is which map is most useful, not which one is the coast. In the information-theoretic view, **full reality** is the unknown and infinitely complex process that actually generated the data ([[Models as Abstractions]]). Ecological systems involve countless small influences, so a "true model" would have nearly infinite parameters, and no model in the candidate set is assumed to equal it.

Information-theoretic selection therefore ranks approximations to reality by their [[Kullback-Leibler Information]] distance from it. One consequence is that as [[Sample Size|sample size]] grows it is reasonable to support more complex models, since more of reality's small effects become detectable, and [[AIC]] behaves this way. [[BIC]]'s usual justification, by contrast, assumes a true model is among the candidates, a difference explored in [[AIC vs BIC]] ([[Anderson and Burnham 2002]]).

The best AIC model is not the true model; it is the best approximation in the set.

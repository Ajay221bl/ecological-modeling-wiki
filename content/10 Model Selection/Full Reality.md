# Full Reality

**In one line:** in the information-theoretic view, full reality is the unknown and infinitely complex process that actually generated the data, which no model in the candidate set is assumed to equal.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Models as Abstractions]] (why no model is true) · [[Kullback-Leibler Information]] (distance from it)

## The everyday picture
A coastline. Its true length depends on the size of your ruler and keeps growing as the ruler shrinks. Every map is a simplification. The question is which map is most useful, not which one is the coast.

## Start from zero
1. **Ecological systems involve countless small influences.**
2. **So the "true model" would have nearly infinite parameters.**
3. **Information-theoretic selection doesn't assume the truth is in the set.** It ranks approximations to it.
4. **Consequence:** as sample size grows, it is reasonable to support more complex models, since more of reality's small effects become detectable. AIC behaves this way.
5. **Contrast with BIC,** whose usual justification assumes a true model is among the candidates.

## In the course
[[Anderson and Burnham 2002]].

## Common confusions
- **"The best AIC model is the true model."** It is the best approximation in the set.

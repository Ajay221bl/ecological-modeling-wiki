# Theme and Key Points

**In one line:** a paper's theme is the single thread running through it, carried by two or three points you want every reader to remember, and anything that does not serve them should be cut.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Final Project]]

## The everyday picture
A necklace. The theme is the string; sentences and paragraphs are beads. A bead that is not on the string is just lying on the table.

## Start from zero
1. **McCarthy's advice (Savage and Yeh 2019):** decide on your theme and two or three points every reader should remember.
2. **Write them down first,** in one sentence each, before drafting.
3. **Test every paragraph:** does it help the reader understand the theme? If not, omit it.
4. **For a modeling paper,** the points are usually the ecological answer, its size and uncertainty, and what it means.
5. **The abstract, introduction's last paragraph, results' first sentences and discussion's first paragraph** should all state the same points.

## Common confusions
- **"Every analysis I ran belongs in the paper."** Only what serves the theme; the rest goes to supplements or the bin.

## Explain it back

*Pass test:* you can state your project's theme and key points in three sentences.

# Catch per Unit Effort

**In one line:** catch per unit effort is the number of animals caught per standard amount of effort, such as fish per net-night, used as an index of abundance.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Population Index]] · [[Capture Probability]]

## The everyday picture
Fish caught per hour of angling. If the lake empties, catch per hour should drop, unless the remaining fish crowd around your favourite spot.

## A tiny example
Powell and Gale (2015 Ch7): a net catches 350 fish in each of three size classes. If the middle class has capture probability 0.9 and the others 0.1, true numbers are about 389 middle-sized fish but 3500 in each of the other classes. Equal catches hid a ninefold difference.

## Start from zero
1. **CPUE = catch / effort.** Effort might be net-nights, trap-nights or angler-hours.
2. **It assumes constant catchability.** Gear selectivity (mesh size) breaks that across sizes.
3. **Hyperstability:** CPUE stays high while abundance falls (fish crowd where the nets are).
4. **Hyperdepletion:** CPUE falls faster than abundance (fish move away from nets).
5. **Harley et al. (2001)** found the CPUE to abundance relation differs among species (cited in Powell and Gale Ch14).

## In the course
[[Powell and Gale 2015]] Ch7 and Ch14.

## Common confusions
- **"Equal catches mean equal numbers."** Only if capture probabilities are equal.

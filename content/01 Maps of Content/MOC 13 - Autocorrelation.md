# MOC 13 - Autocorrelation

Unit 9 (Oct 1): when observations close in space or time resemble each other, ordinary models overstate how much they know. Readings: Diniz-Filho et al. 2003, Dynamic Ecology 'Autocorrelation: friend or foe?'.

- [[Phylogenetic Autocorrelation]]: Phylogenetic autocorrelation means closely related species have similar traits because they share recent ancestors, so species are not independent data points.
- [[Tobler's First Law]]: Tobler's first law of geography says \"everything is related to everything else, but near things are more related than distant things\".
- [[Positive Autocorrelation]]: Positive autocorrelation means nearby observations are more similar than random pairs, producing patches or smooth runs.
- [[Negative Autocorrelation]]: Negative autocorrelation means nearby observations are less similar than random pairs, producing an alternating or evenly spaced pattern.

- [[Isotropy]]: Spatial correlation is isotropic when it depends only on the distance between two points, not on their direction.
- [[Lag]]: A lag is the separation, in time steps or distance, between two observations whose values are being compared.
- [[Time Series]]: A time series is a sequence of measurements of the same thing taken in time order, usually at regular intervals.

- [[Spatial Scale]]: Spatial scale describes the size of the units and of the area in a study, and patterns or processes can change when either changes.
- [[Grain]]: Grain is the size of the smallest unit of observation in a study, such as a 1 m² quadrat or a 50 km grid cell.
- [[Extent]]: Extent is the total area (or time span) a study covers.

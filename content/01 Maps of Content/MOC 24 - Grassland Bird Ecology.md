# MOC 24 - Grassland Bird Ecology

> [!tip] New to this?
> Every note here starts from zero. If a biology word still feels unfamiliar (habitat, niche, community, succession), read [[MOC 19 - Ecology Vocabulary|MOC - Ecology from Zero]] first; for numbers, [[MOC 01 - Basics from Zero|MOC - Basics from Zero]].

Grassland birds have declined more steeply than almost any other bird group in North America. Their habitat is being lost, broken into small pieces, and invaded by trees and shrubs. My project asks whether bird richness at each survey point is better explained by the habitat around the point (tree cover, scrub cover, closeness to a tree-grass boundary) or by which of five Arkansas properties it is on (final proposal, Oct 12). The Oct 13 answer: both matter. All species rose toward tree-grass edges, while the six focal grassland species rose toward cores and fell with tree cover ([[Preliminary Results]]).

## 1. The problem
- [[Grassland Bird Decline]]: the conservation context ([[Rosenberg et al. 2019]])
- [[Woody Encroachment]]: Woody encroachment is trees and shrubs slowly spreading into grassland, turning open prairie into shrubland or woods.
- [[Habitat Fragmentation]]: grassland broken into small, separated patches
- [[Edge Effects]]: why the edges of a patch are worse habitat than its middle
- [[Grassland Core Habitat]]: the open interior, away from trees, that grassland specialists need (in Arkansas it can be small)

## 2. How to measure it
- [[Species Richness]]: my response variable
- [[Spatial Covariance]]: the remote-sensing metric from [[Berry et al. 2026]]
- [[Regime Shifts and Spatial Early Warning Signals]]: the theory behind that metric

## 3. The six focal species
- [[Focal Species]]: why the original study picked these six
- [[Grassland Specialists vs Generalists]]: why the six and "all birds" can tell different stories
- [[Dickcissel]]: the most common focal species in my data
- [[Northern Bobwhite]]: a declining game bird ([[Brennan and Kuvlesky 2005]])
- [[Eastern Meadowlark]]: the open-field singer, mostly at BK (open grass and farmland)
- [[Scissor-tailed Flycatcher]]: the long-tailed flycatcher of open country
- [[Eastern Kingbird]]: the "tyrant" flycatcher, rare in my data
- [[Loggerhead Shrike]]: the songbird that hunts like a hawk, rarest in my data

## 4. How birds use space and time
- [[Habitat Selection]]: birds choose where to settle
- [[Area Sensitivity]]: some species need big patches, not just any grass
- [[Breeding Season Timing]]: why counts happen May to July, and why CR's mid-May dates matter

## 5. Managing grasslands
- [[Grassland Restoration and Management]]: the toolkit used at all five sites
- [[Prescribed Fire]]: planned burns that push back trees
- [[Mowing]]: cutting vegetation short
- [[Disking]]: breaking up soil to reset plants
- [[Defend the Core]]: protect intact grassland first

## 6. The bigger picture
- [[Ecological Resilience and Alternative States]]: grassland and forest as two stable states
- [[Regime Shift]]: A regime shift is when an ecosystem flips into a different, lasting state, such as grassland to juniper woodland, held in place by its own feedbacks and hard to reverse.
- [[Spatial Early Warning Signal]]: A spatial early warning signal is a change in the map pattern of an ecosystem, such as trees clumping and grass neighbourhoods breaking up, that hints a regime shift is coming before the average looks alarming.
- [[Land-Use Change and Agriculture]]: why most grassland disappeared
- [[Climate Change Effects on Wildlife]]: Climate change affects wildlife through shifts in range, timing, body size, survival, reproduction and disease, which ecologists detect by modelling long time series...
- [[Rangeland Health]]: judging how well grassland is doing ([[Batpurev et al. 2021]])

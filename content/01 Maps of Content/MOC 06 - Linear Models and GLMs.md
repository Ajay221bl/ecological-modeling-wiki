# MOC 06 - Linear Models and GLMs

Unit 4 (Sep 1, 3): the straight-line model, how to read it, and how GLMs extend it to counts, proportions and yes/no data. Readings: Guthery and Bingham 2007, Hobbs and Hooten 2015 Ch2, Zuur et al. 2009 Ch2.

## 1. The straight-line model
- [[Linear Regression]]: Linear regression describes how the average of a numeric response changes with a predictor by fitting the straight line that comes closest to the data.
- [[Linear Function]]: A linear function is a rule of the form y = a + b·x, whose graph is a straight line that rises or falls by the same amount for every one-unit step in x.
- [[Intercept]]: The intercept is the model's predicted response when every predictor equals zero.
- [[Slope]]: The slope is the change in the expected response for a one-unit increase in a predictor.
- [[Regression Coefficient]]: A regression coefficient is one of the numbers a model multiplies its predictors by, estimated from the data, that says how the response changes with that...
- [[Fitted Value]]: A fitted value is the model's prediction for an observation that was used to fit it: the height of the fitted line at that observation's predictor values.
- [[Residual]]: A residual is the difference between what was observed and what the model fitted for that observation.
- [[Error Term]]: The error term is the part of each observation that the deterministic part of the model doesn't account for, treated as a random draw from a distribution.
- [[Least Squares]]: Least squares is the method of choosing the line that makes the sum of the squared residuals as small as possible.
- [[Residual Standard Error]]: The residual standard error is the estimated standard deviation of the scatter of observations around the fitted line.
- [[lm()]]: `lm()` is the R function that fits a linear model by least squares from a formula and a data frame.
- [[Multiple Regression]]: Multiple regression is linear regression with two or more predictors, each given its own coefficient.
- [[Holding Other Variables Constant]]: Holding other variables constant means comparing units that differ in one predictor but are alike in all the other predictors in the model.
- [[Interpreting Regression Coefficients]]: Interpreting a regression coefficient means turning the number into a plain statement about how the expected response differs between units, with its units,...
- [[Effect Size]]: An effect size is how big a difference or relationship is, expressed in units that mean something.

## 2. Categories and interactions
- [[Dummy Coding]]: Dummy coding turns a categorical predictor into columns of 0s and 1s so that it can be used in a regression equation.
- [[Reference Level]]: The reference level is the category of a factor that a model uses as its baseline, against which every other category's coefficient is measured.
- [[Sum-to-zero Contrasts]]: Sum-to-zero contrasts code a factor so that the intercept is the average of the group means and each coefficient is one group's deviation from that average.
- [[Interaction Term]]: An interaction term lets the effect of one predictor depend on the value of another.
- [[Model Matrix]]: The model matrix is the table of numbers a model actually works with: one row per observation and one column per coefficient.
- [[ANOVA]]: Analysis of variance compares the means of several groups by asking whether the variation between group means is larger than expected from the variation...
- [[Two-way ANOVA]]: Two-way ANOVA is a linear model with two categorical predictors, optionally with their interaction.
- [[ANCOVA]]: ANCOVA is a linear model that combines a categorical predictor with a continuous one, comparing groups after adjusting for the continuous covariate.

## 3. Judging and testing a fit
- [[R-squared]]: R-squared is the proportion of the variation in the response that the model's fitted values account for.
- [[Adjusted R-squared]]: Adjusted R-squared is R-squared with a penalty for the number of predictors, so that it rises only when a new predictor improves the fit by more than chance...
- [[t-test]]: A t-test judges whether an estimate differs from a reference value, usually zero, by dividing the difference by the estimate's standard error and comparing...
- [[F-test]]: An F-test judges whether a group of terms improves a linear model by comparing the drop in residual variation they produce with the residual variation that...
- [[Residual Sum of Squares]]: the residual sum of squares is the total of the squared residuals, Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)², measuring how much variation a fitted model leaves unexplained.
- [[Total Sum of Squares]]: the total sum of squares is Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)², the total variation of the response around its mean before any predictor is used.

# MOC 19 - Ecology Vocabulary

The ecology words the readings, labs and homework datasets assume you know, each explained from nothing.

## 1. Ecology from zero
The first ideas, in the order to read them: what ecology studies, the levels it works at, and how communities, ecosystems and grasslands change.

- [[Ecology]]: Ecology is the science of where living things are, how many there are, and why, worked out by studying how they interact with each other and with their surroundings.
- [[Levels of Ecological Organization]]: Ecologists look at nature at nested levels, from one organism up to the whole planet, and each level asks a different kind of question.
- [[Species and Scientific Names]]: A species is a kind of organism whose members can breed with each other, and its scientific name is a two-part label (genus plus species) that means the same thing to everyone, everywhere.
- [[Ecological Community]]: An ecological community is all the populations of different species that live in the same place at the same time and can affect each other.
- [[Brood Parasitism]]: Brood parasitism is when one bird lays its eggs in another species' nest and leaves the host to raise its young, as the Brown-headed Cowbird does.
- [[Ecological Niche]]: A species' niche is its way of making a living: the range of conditions it can tolerate, the resources it uses, and the role it plays among other species.
- [[Habitat Specialist]]: A habitat specialist is a species that can only make a living in a narrow kind of place, such as open grassland, so it suffers most when that place shrinks or changes.
- [[Habitat Generalist]]: A habitat generalist is a species that can make a living in many kinds of places, such as woods, hedges, yards and fields, so it copes well, and often gains, when habitats change.
- [[Ecosystem]]: An ecosystem is a community of living things together with its non-living surroundings (sunlight, water, soil, weather, fire), linked by flows of energy and the recycling of materials.
- [[Biome]]: A biome is a huge region of the Earth with a similar climate and the same general type of vegetation, such as desert, grassland or deciduous forest.
- [[Ecoregions]]: Ecoregions are large areas of land that share a similar mix of climate, landforms, soils, water and natural vegetation, so that ecosystems inside one region behave more like each other than like those outside it.
- [[Grassland]]: A grassland is open land dominated by grasses and wildflowers, with few or no trees, kept that way by dry climate, fire, grazing, mowing, or a mix of these.
- [[Biodiversity]]: Biodiversity is the variety of life, counted at several levels: genes within a species, species within a place, and kinds of ecosystems across a region.
- [[Disturbance]]: A disturbance is a fairly sudden event, such as a fire, flood, mowing or ploughing, that kills or removes living things and frees up space, light and nutrients for others.
- [[Ecological Succession]]: Succession is the gradual, fairly predictable change in which plants (and so which animals) occupy a place after it is cleared, typically from weeds to grass to shrubs to forest in a humid climate.
- [[Feedback Loops in Ecology]]: A feedback loop is a chain of cause and effect that comes back around to affect its own starting point, either pushing the system back toward where it was (negative feedback) or further away (positive feedback).
- [[Tipping Point]]: A tipping point is the threshold at which a small extra push makes a system flip to a different state, because a feedback loop takes over and carries the change on by itself.
- [[Ecological Resilience and Alternative States]]: Alternative states are the different stable kinds of system (such as grassland or woodland) that the same land can settle into; resilience is how hard you can push a system before it tips from one to the other.
- [[Landscape Ecology]]: Landscape ecology is the part of ecology that asks how the arrangement of things on the ground (where fields, woods, roads and rivers sit, and how big they are) changes what lives there.

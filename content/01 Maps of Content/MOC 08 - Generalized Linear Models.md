# MOC 08 - Generalized Linear Models

Unit 4 (Sep 3 lab and Homework 2): the linear model stretched to counts, proportions and yes/no data with a distribution and a link function. Readings: Zuur et al. 2009 Ch8 to 10.

## 1. The three parts of a GLM
- [[Generalized Linear Model]]: A generalized linear model extends linear regression to responses such as counts and yes/no outcomes by pairing a suitable probability distribution with a...
- [[Linear Predictor]]: The linear predictor is the weighted sum of predictors, β₀ + β₁x₁ + β₂x₂ + …, that a GLM computes before converting it to a mean with the inverse link.
- [[Link Function]]: A link function is the transformation applied to the mean of the response so that the result can be modeled as a straight-line combination of predictors.
- [[Inverse Link Function]]: The inverse link function converts a value of the linear predictor back into a mean on the scale of the data.
- [[Link Scale]]: The link scale is the transformed scale on which a GLM's linear predictor lives and on which its coefficients add up.
- [[Response Scale]]: The response scale is the scale of the data themselves, such as counts or probabilities, onto which GLM results are converted with the inverse link for...
- [[Identity Link]]: The identity link leaves the mean unchanged, so the mean itself is modeled as a linear function of the predictors.
- [[Log Link]]: The log link models the logarithm of the mean as a linear function of predictors, which keeps the mean positive and makes effects multiplicative.
- [[Rate Ratio]]: A rate ratio is the factor by which the expected count is multiplied for a one-unit increase in a predictor, obtained by exponentiating a log-link coefficient.
- [[Logit Link]]: The logit link models the log-odds of a probability as a linear function of predictors, stretching the interval from 0 to 1 over the whole number line.
- [[plogis()]]: `plogis()` converts log-odds to probabilities and `qlogis()` converts probabilities to log-odds.
- [[Probit Link]]: The probit link converts a probability into the z-score that has that much of the standard normal distribution below it, and models that z-score as a linear...
- [[Complementary Log-log Link]]: The complementary log-log link is an asymmetric link for probabilities, log(−log(1 − p)), that arises when a yes/no outcome records whether a count is above...

- [[Poisson Regression]]: Poisson regression models a count response as Poisson-distributed with a mean that depends on predictors through a log link.

- [[Underdispersion]]: Underdispersion means the data vary less than the model's distribution predicts, as when counts have a variance smaller than their mean under a Poisson model.
- [[Negative Binomial GLM]]: A negative binomial GLM is a count regression like Poisson regression with one extra parameter that lets the variance exceed the mean.

- [[Hurdle Model]]: A hurdle model splits count data into two separate questions: whether the count is zero or not, and how large it is given that it is above zero.
- [[Gamma GLM]]: A gamma GLM models a positive, continuous, right-skewed response whose spread grows in proportion to its mean, usually with a log link.

## 4. Measuring fit with deviance
- [[Deviance]]: Deviance is a GLM's measure of misfit: twice the gap in log-likelihood between a perfect-fit model and your model, so smaller means closer to the data.
- [[Saturated Model]]: A saturated model has as many parameters as observations, so it reproduces every data point exactly and marks the best log-likelihood any model could achieve.
- [[Null Deviance]]: Null deviance is the deviance of the intercept-only model, the misfit you have before using any predictor.
- [[Residual Deviance]]: Residual deviance is the deviance left over after fitting your model, the GLM counterpart of the residual sum of squares.
- [[Deviance Explained]]: Deviance explained is the fraction of the null deviance that a GLM's predictors remove, a rough counterpart of R-squared.

# Strong Inference

**In one line:** strong inference is Platt's (1964) method of progress in science: list alternative hypotheses, design a test that can exclude some of them, run it, and repeat with what survives.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Hypothesis]] (what is tested) · [[Hypothetico-deductive Method]] (the general pattern) · [[Multiple Working Hypotheses]] (its starting point)

## The everyday picture
A tournament bracket. Each round is designed so that some contenders must lose. Round after round the field narrows, and progress is fast because every match eliminates someone.

## Start from zero
1. **Devise alternative hypotheses.**
2. **Devise a crucial experiment** whose possible outcomes would exclude one or more of them.
3. **Carry it out cleanly** to get a clear result.
4. **Recycle:** refine the surviving hypotheses and repeat.
5. **Platt argued** that fields that work this way (molecular biology at the time) advance much faster than fields that test one favoured idea at a time.
6. **In ecology** crucial experiments are often impossible. Model selection is a substitute: each hypothesis becomes a model, and the data weigh them all at once.
7. **The spirit carries over:** compare explicit alternatives, rather than testing one idea against "nothing happens".

## In the course

## Common confusions
- **"Strong inference means strong statistical evidence."** It is a strategy for designing research, not a measure of evidence.

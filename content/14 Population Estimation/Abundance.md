# Abundance

The number of people in a stadium is not the number you can see from your seat, and not the tickets sold last week, but the number in the stadium right now. **Abundance** N is the number of individuals of a species in a defined area at a defined time ([[Biological Population]], [[Count Data]]). It needs boundaries, an area and a time window. **Density** is abundance per unit area, such as birds per hectare, which allows comparison of areas of different size.

True abundance is a [[Latent Variable|latent quantity]], and counts estimate it only after correcting for the area sampled and for detection ([[Count-based Abundance Estimation]], [[Imperfect Detection]]). The estimation methods in this folder include the [[Lincoln-Petersen Estimator]], the [[Schnabel Estimator]], [[Removal Sampling]], [[Double-observer Methods]], the [[N-mixture Model]] and [[Distance Sampling]]. Relative abundance, an index, compares places or years without claiming the true N ([[Population Index]]).

Powell and Gale (2015 Ch7, Ch8 and Ch14) ask "What is the difference between abundance and density?" ([[Powell and Gale 2015]], [[Census]]). Abundance and density are not the same: density divides abundance by area.

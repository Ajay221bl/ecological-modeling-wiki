# Breeding Amphibian Survey

Adult frogs can hide well, but a clump of eggs in a pond in spring is hard to miss and proves frogs bred there. A **breeding amphibian survey** detects frogs, toads and salamanders from evidence of breeding, such as egg masses, tadpoles or larvae, found by visual search, dipnetting, stream searches or snorkelling ([[Occupancy]], [[Imperfect Detection]]). The guest lecture lists it among the survey types, "looking for evidence of breeding".

Because the evidence only exists for part of the year, the survey season must match the species' breeding window, and the occupancy then means "used for breeding", which is narrower than "present" ([[Closure Assumption]], [[Availability for Detection]]). Detection depends on water clarity, depth, vegetation and searcher effort, which go in p as [[Observation Covariates]]. [[Repeat-Visit Design|Repeat visits]] must be close enough that eggs or larvae are still there, but separate enough to be independent looks.

It is one of the survey types in the [[Occupancy Guest Lecture]] ([[Choosing a Survey Method for Occupancy]]). Finding no eggs does not mean no frogs: adults may live at a site that failed to breed that year, so the method answers a breeding question, not a presence question.

# Track Survey

After a snowfall you can tell which animals crossed a field overnight without seeing a single one. A **track survey** detects a species from its footprints, along transects in snow or mud, or on track plates, sooty or inked surfaces with paper that record prints of animals passing over them ([[Occupancy]], [[Imperfect Detection]]). The guest lecture notes these are "usually for mammals", done along transects, and that "winter is best time" and "timing is important": fresh snow resets the slate, so tracks found the next day belong to a known window.

Detection depends on conditions: snow age, temperature, wind and the observer's skill at telling similar species apart, so these are [[Observation Covariates]] for p. Track plates need "a substrate that holds tracks" and regular visits, and are "sensitive to weather and identification"; the lecture says they have been "largely replaced by camera traps" ([[Camera Trap]]). Misidentified tracks create false positives, which the standard occupancy model assumes never happen ([[Single-season Occupancy]]).

It is one of the survey types in the [[Occupancy Guest Lecture]] ([[Choosing a Survey Method for Occupancy]]). Repeat transects are only valid repeat visits if the tracks are new each time; tracks that persist from one visit to the next are the same detection counted twice ([[Site Independence Assumption]]).

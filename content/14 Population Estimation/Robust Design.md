# Robust Design

Taking the class register several times in one lesson, and doing so every week of term, gives closed periods within lessons and open periods between weeks. The **robust design** combines closed and open sampling: several closely spaced secondary occasions within each primary period, treated as closed, and longer gaps between primary periods, which are open ([[Closed Population]], [[Cormack-Jolly-Seber Model]], [[Primary and Secondary Sampling Periods]]). Pollock (1982) proposed it.

Within primary periods, closed-capture models estimate N_t and p; between them, open models estimate survival. [[Temporary Emigration]] is estimable because detection within periods is separated from presence. It is robust to unequal catchability in the closed part, hence the name ([[Capture Heterogeneity Models]]). [[Multi-season Occupancy]] has the same structure at the species level.

It is covered in [[Powell and Gale 2015]] Ch13 ([[Super-population]]). The robust design is not just more occasions: their arrangement into closed and open periods is what adds estimable parameters.

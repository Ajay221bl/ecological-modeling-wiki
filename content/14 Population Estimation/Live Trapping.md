# Live Trapping

Some animals are almost never seen or heard: mice in leaf litter, fish under water. The only reliable way to know they are there is to catch one. **Live trapping** detects a species by catching individuals in traps, nets or by electrofishing, and for occupancy a single capture at a site on a visit counts as a detection, a 1 in the [[Detection History]] ([[Occupancy]], [[Imperfect Detection]]). The guest lecture lists small-mammal traps, gill nets and electroshocking for fish, and notes there is "no need to mark animals": occupancy only needs to know that the species was there, not which individual.

The lecture also calls trapping "relatively inefficient": traps must be set, checked and closed, every check costs a visit, and handling stresses animals. Its strength is for animals not detectable by other methods. Detection depends on trap effort (number of traps and nights), bait, weather and trap-shyness, so those belong in the detection part of the model as [[Observation Covariates]]. If you do mark the animals, the same field work can feed a [[Mark-Recapture]] study of abundance and survival.

It is one of the survey types in the [[Occupancy Guest Lecture]] ([[Choosing a Survey Method for Occupancy]]). Trapping for occupancy is not the same design as trapping for mark-recapture: occupancy needs many sites each visited a few times, while mark-recapture needs many marked individuals caught repeatedly ([[Mark-Recapture vs Unmarked Survey Methods]]).

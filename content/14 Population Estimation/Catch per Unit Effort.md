# Catch per Unit Effort

If a lake empties, the fish you catch per hour of angling should drop, unless the remaining fish crowd around your favourite spot. **Catch per unit effort** (CPUE) is the number of animals caught per standard amount of effort, such as fish per net-night, used as an index of abundance ([[Population Index]]). CPUE = catch / effort, where effort might be net-nights, trap-nights or angler-hours. It assumes constant catchability, and gear selectivity such as mesh size breaks that across sizes ([[Capture Probability]]).

Powell and Gale (2015 Ch7) give an example: a net catches 350 fish in each of three size classes. If the middle class has capture probability 0.9 and the others 0.1, the true numbers are about 350 / 0.9 ≈ 389 middle-sized fish but 350 / 0.1 = 3,500 in each other class, so equal catches hid a ninefold difference. Hyperstability is CPUE staying high while abundance falls, because fish crowd where the nets are; hyperdepletion is CPUE falling faster than abundance, because fish move away from nets.

Harley et al. (2001), cited in Powell and Gale Ch14, found that the CPUE-abundance relation differs among species ([[Powell and Gale 2015]] Ch7 and Ch14). Equal catches mean equal numbers only if capture probabilities are equal.

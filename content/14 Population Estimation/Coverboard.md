# Coverboard

On a cool morning a snake looks for a warm, sheltered spot, and a sheet of plywood or metal that has been sitting in the sun is perfect. A **coverboard** is a flat board laid on the ground that reptiles and amphibians shelter under, which surveyors lift on each visit to check for the species ([[Occupancy]], [[Detection History]]). The guest lecture describes it as "specific to herps" that "takes advantage of thermal needs": the board warms up and holds moisture, so cold-blooded animals gather under it and become easy to find.

Each lift is a visit, so a site with several boards checked several times gives a natural detection history. Detection depends strongly on temperature, time of day and recent rain, which are [[Observation Covariates]] for p, and boards usually need weeks to "season" before animals use them. Because animals are in hand, coverboard surveys can be combined with marking for [[Mark-Recapture]] studies, as the lecture notes.

It is one of the survey types in the [[Occupancy Guest Lecture]] ([[Choosing a Survey Method for Occupancy]]). A board with nothing under it on a hot afternoon says little: in heat the animals may be elsewhere, which is a miss, not an absence ([[Imperfect Detection]]).

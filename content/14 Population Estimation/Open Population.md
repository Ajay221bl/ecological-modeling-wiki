# Open Population

**In one line:** an open population is one where births, deaths, immigration or emigration happen between sampling occasions, so models estimate rates of change such as survival rather than one fixed size.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Closed Population]] · [[Survival Rate]]

## The everyday picture
A café over a whole day. Customers come and go; you can estimate how long people stay and how many arrive, but not "the" number of customers.

## Start from zero
1. **Studies run longer** than closed ones, and we want deaths to happen, or survival cannot be estimated (Powell and Gale Ch7).
2. **Open models:** [[Cormack-Jolly-Seber Model]] (survival), [[Jolly-Seber and POPAN Models]] (also recruitment and N), [[Dead Recovery Models]], [[Multi-state Models]].
3. **"Short" and "long" are species-relative.** A closed study of elephants can last longer than an open study of mice.
4. **A 0 in an open-model history can mean dead, emigrated or missed.**

## In the course
[[Powell and Gale 2015]] Ch7 and Ch10.

## Common confusions
- **"Open models can still give N easily."** CJS conditions on marked animals and does not estimate N; Jolly-Seber types are needed.

## Explain it back
- Why do open-population studies need deaths to happen?

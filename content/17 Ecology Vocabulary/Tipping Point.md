# Tipping Point

Lean back on a chair. Tilt a little and it rocks back to all four legs; tilt a little further and, past one particular angle, it falls over by itself. That angle is a **tipping point**: the threshold at which a small extra push makes a system flip to a different state, because past it a [[Feedback Loops in Ecology|feedback loop]] (a chain of cause and effect that comes back to its start) stops pushing the system back and starts carrying the change on by itself. Before the tipping point the system recovers from small pushes; after it, it runs away to the other state.

See it in a grassland. Each year fire, fuelled by grass, removes young trees. Take a toy rule: at 10% tree cover there is plenty of grass fuel and cover returns to about 10% the next year; at 30% fuel is lower and cover creeps up to 33%; at 50% fires are weak and cover jumps to 60%. Somewhere between 10% and 30% lies the tipping point: below it the grass-fire loop wins and the field stays grassy, above it the tree-shade loop wins and the field heads for woodland (invented numbers; real thresholds depend on soil, climate and fire). Crossing a tipping point is what turns a gradual change into a [[Regime Shift]], a flip to a lasting new state.

Two confusions. A tipping point is not the point where change becomes visible: the system can look fine right up to it, which is why ecologists look for a [[Spatial Early Warning Signal]] in the map pattern before the average looks alarming. And crossing back is not as easy as crossing over: once the new state's feedbacks hold, returning takes far more effort, the reason [[Defend the Core]] protects intact grassland first ([[Woody Encroachment]], [[Ecological Resilience and Alternative States]]).

# Phenology

The date your street's cherry tree blossoms each spring comes earlier in warmer springs. **Phenology** is the timing of recurring life-cycle events, such as flowering, migration, egg laying or spring green-up, and how it shifts with climate ([[Time Series]]). It is measured as the [[Dates in R|Day of Year]], or Julian day, of an event. Climate warming advances many spring events, and range shifts, phenology shifts and smaller [[Body Size|body size]] are the predicted universal responses (cited in [[Shipley et al. 2022]]).

When predator and prey shift timing at different rates, food peaks and breeding peaks drift apart, a mismatch ([[Climate Change Effects on Wildlife]]). Phenology is modelled with GAMs, using cyclic smooths for season, or with date as the response in [[Mixed Effects Model|mixed models]] ([[Cyclic Spline]], [[Generalized Additive Model]]). NDVI green-up curves are a satellite view of plant phenology, and [[Lawton et al. 2022]] used NDVI with locust data ([[NDVI]]).

Phenology is not the same as season: it is the timing of biological events, which can shift within the same season.

# Latitudinal Gradient

A **latitudinal gradient** is a systematic change in a biological pattern with latitude, most famously the increase in [[Species Richness]] from the poles to the tropics ([[Environmental Gradient]]). Latitude is a proxy, not a cause: temperature, energy, season length and history all change with it. [[Diniz-Filho et al. 2003]] found that bird richness showed a quadratic north-south gradient across their study region.

[[Christensen et al. 2020]] found the effect of drought on haemorrhagic disease depended on latitude, a drought × latitude interaction, with disease reports expanding northward ([[Interaction Term]], [[Drought Index]]). As a [[Predictor Variable|covariate]], latitude often absorbs broad spatial trends, like a trend surface ([[Spatial Smooths]]).

Bergmann's rule in [[Shipley et al. 2022]] is another latitudinal pattern ([[Body Size]], [[Macroecology]]). Latitude does not explain richness: it describes where richness changes, and the explanation lies in the variables that vary with latitude.

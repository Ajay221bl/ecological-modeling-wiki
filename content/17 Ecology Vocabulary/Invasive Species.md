# Invasive Species

Most plants carried to a new continent in a garden or a bag of seed never escape the garden. A few take off and cover whole regions. An **invasive species** is one brought by people to a region where it did not evolve (an exotic, introduced or non-native species) that then spreads widely and causes ecological or economic harm. "Noxious weed" is the legal label for an invasive plant that landowners must control. The surprising part is how variable the outcome is: the same species can be a pest in one state and a minor roadside plant in the next.

Bull thistle, Cirsium vulgare, is the example in Homework 4. It came from Eurasia and is a serious invader in much of the United States, but in Nebraska it stays rare even after many decades. One explanation for invasions in general is that the invader left its natural enemies behind; one explanation for Nebraska's exception is that native insects that eat the native tall thistle also eat bull thistle seeds there ([[Enemy Release Hypothesis]]).

Testing that idea needs an experiment, because places with and without herbivores differ in many other ways. The Homework 4 design sows a fixed number of seeds, removes insects with insecticide in half the plots, and varies the distance to a native thistle patch, so seedling counts can be compared across both treatments ([[Study Design|Experimental Design]], [[Homework 4 - Thistle Seedlings and Enemy Release]]).

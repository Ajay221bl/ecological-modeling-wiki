# Species Interaction

A cat in the garden changes where the birds feed, and two cafés on one street compete for the same customers. A **species interaction** is any way one species affects another's survival, behaviour or distribution, such as predation (one eats the other), competition (both use a limited resource), or facilitation (one helps the other) ([[Population]], [[Habitat]]). Interactions are often drawn as arrows marked + or −: a puma with a − arrow to a fox means pumas reduce foxes, by killing them or scaring them away.

Interactions can shape where species occur as much as habitat does. A smaller predator may avoid places where a larger one lives, even when the habitat suits it, and then appears "rare" in good habitat for a reason a habitat model cannot see. The guest lecture's example uses two foxes (chilla and culpeo), pumas and humans: the larger carnivore's presence lowers the chance of finding the smaller ones, which multi-species occupancy models measure as a difference in [[Conditional Occupancy]] ([[Multi-species Occupancy]], [[Co-occurrence Probability]]).

It is the reason for the multi-species slides in the [[Occupancy Guest Lecture]]: "biotic and community interactions are important!" Co-occurring less often than expected is consistent with an interaction, but it does not prove one: the two species may simply prefer different habitats ([[Correlation vs Causation]]).

# Habitat Patch

Picture a field of wheat with a few woods scattered across it like islands in a sea. Each wood is a **habitat patch**: a separate piece of suitable habitat surrounded by land the species does not use much. Patches have their own properties, such as area and perimeter, and those are called patch or "local" variables. A bigger patch holds more food and more territories; a long thin patch has more edge per hectare than a round one, and edges are windier, brighter and closer to predators ([[Edge Effects|Edge Effect]]).

[[Landscape Ecology|Landscape ecology]] adds a second level of description: what is around the patch. How much woodland lies within 2 km, how many other patches there are, how far away the nearest one is. Those are landscape variables, and they are measured in a circle, a buffer, around the patch at a chosen radius ([[Landscape Metrics]], [[Spatial Scale]]). The core question of Homework 3 is whether woodland birds respond more to the patch they are in or to the landscape around it ([[Patch vs Landscape Effects]]).

In the Mt Lofty data, PA (patch area), PP (patch perimeter) and MRF (mean annual rainfall) are the three local [[Predictor Variable|covariates]]; everything ending in 2K, 5K or 10K is a landscape covariate at that radius. A patch is not the same as a site in every study: here each patch is one survey site, but a large patch could hold several ([[Sampling Unit]]).

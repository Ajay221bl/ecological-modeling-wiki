# Coarse Woody Debris

**Coarse woody debris** (CWD) is dead wood on the ground, such as fallen logs and large branches above a size threshold, often 7.5 to 10 cm in diameter, which provides habitat and nutrients ([[Habitat Structure]]). It is measured by line-intersect sampling, recording diameters crossing a transect, or by volume per plot. It is habitat for salamanders, small mammals, fungi, invertebrates and, as standing snags, cavity nesters.

It is changed by fire, which can consume or create it, and by logging and storms ([[Fire History Variables]]). Its data are skewed with many zeros, so hurdle or Tweedie models can suit ([[Hurdle Model]], [[Tweedie Distribution]]).

It appears in the lab datasets ([[Course Website]]). Dead wood is not a sign of poor habitat; it is a key resource for many species.

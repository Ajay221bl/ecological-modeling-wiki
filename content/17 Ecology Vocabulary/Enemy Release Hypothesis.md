# Enemy Release Hypothesis

**In one line:** the enemy release hypothesis proposes that introduced species thrive because they have left behind the predators, parasites and pathogens that limited them in their native range.

> [!info] Before this, it helps to know
> [[Hypothesis]]

## Start from zero
1. **Prediction:** fewer enemies (herbivore damage, parasites) on a species in its introduced range than in its native range.
2. **Second prediction:** fewer enemies on the invader than on native relatives in the same place.
3. **Testing it** compares counts or damage between ranges or species, often with GLMMs (sites as random effects).
4. **Support is mixed;** release from enemies is common, but whether it causes invasion success is debated.

## Common confusions
- **"Fewer enemies proves enemy release caused the invasion."** It shows release, not that release explains success.

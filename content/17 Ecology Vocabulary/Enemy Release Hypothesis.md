# Enemy Release Hypothesis

The **enemy release hypothesis** proposes that [[Invasive Species|introduced species]] thrive because they have left behind the predators, parasites and pathogens that limited them in their native range ([[Hypothesis]]). Its first prediction is fewer enemies, such as herbivore damage or parasites, on a species in its introduced range than in its native range; its second is fewer enemies on the invader than on native relatives in the same place.

Testing it compares counts or damage between ranges or species, often with GLMMs that treat sites as [[Random Effect|random effects]] ([[GLMM]], [[Count Data]]). Support is mixed: release from enemies is common, but whether it causes invasion success is debated.

It is the background to a lab dataset ([[Course Website]]). Fewer enemies does not prove enemy release caused the invasion; it shows release, not that release explains success.

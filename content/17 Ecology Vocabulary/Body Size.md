# Body Size

**Body size and morphology** are an organism's physical dimensions, such as wing length, skull length or mass, which link to physiology, survival and reproduction ([[Continuous Data]], [[Variable]]). Structural size, such as bones, wing and head-bill length, changes little within a season, while mass changes daily ([[Body Condition]]). Bergmann's rule says that within a group body size tends to be larger in colder places, the basis for predicting shrinking under warming ([[Climate Change Effects on Wildlife]]).

[[Shipley et al. 2022]] found that adult tree swallows became structurally smaller over three decades, even though chicks grew larger in warmer nests and larger chicks were more likely to return; adult size trends were associated with warmer overwintering conditions. [[Observation Error|Measurement error]] between observers matters, and repeatability analyses, mixed models with [[Observer Effects|observer effects]], check it ([[Intraclass Correlation]]). Because individuals are measured repeatedly over years, body size is analysed with mixed models ([[Mixed Effects Model]], [[Repeated Measures]]).

It appears in Shipley et al. and the lab datasets ([[Course Website]]). Body size and body mass are not the same: mass also reflects condition and changes quickly.

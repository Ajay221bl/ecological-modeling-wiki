# Habitat Specialist

Think of someone who will only eat at one kind of restaurant. Close half the restaurants in town and they may go hungry. A **habitat specialist** is a species like that: it can only make a living in a narrow kind of [[Habitat]] (the set of resources and conditions where a species lives), because its [[Ecological Niche]], its way of making a living, is narrow. A *grassland specialist* needs open grass to nest and feed and usually avoids trees; the [[Eastern Meadowlark]] is one. Ecologists often split grassland birds further: *obligate* grassland birds depend on grassland for nearly everything, while *facultative* ones also use shrubs, scattered trees or other open habitats (the split is usually traced to Vickery et al. 1999; check which category that source gives each species before quoting it).

Follow one field as trees creep in ([[Woody Encroachment]]). In year 0 it is open grass and holds 3 grassland specialists; once a tree line grows along one side, only 1 remains, because the trees bring predators' perches and shade out the grass the specialists need ([[Edge Effects]]). The specialist has nowhere else to go, so its numbers fall wherever its one kind of place shrinks, which is a large part of why grassland birds have declined more than most other groups ([[Grassland Bird Decline]]). "Specialist" always means specialist *of something*: a Yellow-breasted Chat is a shrubland specialist, so in a grassland study it counts as a non-grassland bird, and its arrival signals shrubs moving in. And it is a sliding scale, not a fixed box: the [[Northern Bobwhite]] needs grass but also shrubby cover, so it sits between specialist and generalist. The opposite end of the scale is the [[Habitat Generalist]].

In the grassland bird project, the six [[Focal Species]] of [[Berry et al. 2026]] are grassland-dependent birds, chosen because specialists are the ones that tell you about grassland quality. They were scarce compared with generalists: Dickcissel was detected at 331 point-visits and Northern Bobwhite at 202, against 863 for the Indigo Bunting, an edge bird ([[Grassland Specialists vs Generalists]]).

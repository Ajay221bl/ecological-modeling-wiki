# Evapotranspiration

**Evapotranspiration** is water lost to the air from soil and plants. Potential evapotranspiration (PET) measures available energy, how much could evaporate, and is high where it is hot and sunny regardless of rain. Actual evapotranspiration (AET) measures how much does evaporate, limited by both energy and water, so it is high in warm, wet places and serves as a productivity proxy ([[Explanatory Variable]], [[Environmental Gradient]]).

Water-energy hypotheses of species richness use AET and PET as predictors ([[Species Richness]]). [[Diniz-Filho et al. 2003]] used PET, AET, range in elevation, annual mean temperature and minimum temperature to explain bird richness in western and central Europe, North Africa and the Middle East, comparing OLS and GLS ([[Generalized Least Squares]]). These predictors are smooth over space, so they carry strong spatial autocorrelation ([[Spatial Autocorrelation]]).

PET is not how much water plants use: that is AET, and PET is the potential if water were unlimited.

# Habitat

**Habitat** is the set of resources and conditions where an organism lives, and [[Habitat Selection|habitat use]] or selection describes how animals use places relative to what is available ([[Predictor Variable|Explanatory Variable]]). Use is where animals are found; selection is use compared with availability. Resource selection functions compare used locations with available ones, often with logistic regression ([[Logistic Regression]]).

Detection matters: a habitat where animals are hard to see looks avoided ([[Why raw counts mislead]], [[Imperfect Detection]]). Scale matters too, since selection at the home-range scale can differ from selection within it ([[Spatial Scale]]). [[Occupancy Model|Occupancy models]] with habitat covariates estimate habitat relationships while correcting for detection (Powell and Gale Ch15) ([[Occupancy]], [[Site Covariates]]).

It is covered in [[Powell and Gale 2015]] Ch15 and the lab datasets ([[Course Website]], [[Habitat Structure]]). Where you find animals is not necessarily their best habitat: use is not quality, and sink habitats can attract animals.

# Course Overview

**Ecological Modeling, Fall 2026.** BIOL580V section 003, 3 credit hours. Taught by Dr. Caleb P. Roberts. Tue and Thu, 2:00 to 3:15 pm, SCEN 606.

## What the course is for
The course samples the statistical topics ecologists use most, so that I can **run and interpret modeling analyses on my own**. The topics are generalized linear models, mixed models, generalized additive models, hierarchical models, and frequentist, information-theoretic and Bayesian methods, all done in R. The syllabus stresses modern methods and study design throughout.

Course objectives. By the end I should be able to:
1. Identify and summarize statistical results as they appear in peer-reviewed articles.
2. Use R to run model-based analyses of ecological data.
3. Combine statistical results with ecological knowledge to support a conclusion.
4. Evaluate how statistical results are presented in writing.

> [!tip] What this means for how I study
> Objectives 1 and 4 are about *reading* statistics, and objectives 2 and 3 are about *doing* them. So every concept note in this wiki has both a "what does this look like in a paper" angle (the *In the course* section, using the assigned papers) and a "how do I run it" angle (the *In R* section).

## Format
About half the sessions are lectures and discussion. The other half are labs for exercises and for fixing modeling and coding problems. The course is not an introduction to R: Dr. Roberts teaches some R, but I'm expected to be proficient already or to teach myself. That's why [[MOC - R for Ecological Modeling]] starts from scratch.
